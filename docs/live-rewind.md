# Live rewind and playback

For the October 2026 playback incident, its fixes, regression coverage and troubleshooting checklist, see the [rewind playback incident report](rewind-playback-incident-2026-10-12.md).

In the viewer menu, select **Enable live rewind buffer**, then enter the desired **History (seconds)** and **Memory per camera (MiB)**. Both settings are saved on the server. There is no fixed maximum history window: enter the duration and capacity suitable for your host. Defaults are 120 seconds and 128 MiB per camera; buffering starts off on an existing installation.

The server captures compressed H.264/H.265 video and available AAC audio from every connected camera in the active configuration, even with no browsers open. It shares the main-stream pipeline with live listening: supported video is copied without decoding or re-encoding, and available audio is converted to AAC. Unsupported H.264 profiles, incompatible MP4 input or oversized fragments can use the H.264 compatibility conversion; administrators can also force conversion with `LiveMedia:ForceTranscoding`. Capture continues while a browser is paused, focused on another camera, or closed. Permanent recording can remain disabled. Rewind media stays in RAM until you explicitly save a selection.

## Controls

A small playback tab protrudes from the left edge just above the bottom camera footers. Moving the mouse into its vicinity reveals a right-pointing chevron; click the tab to open the playback drawer. On a touch screen, the first tap in that small area reveals only the tab, and tapping the tab opens the drawer without changing playback. Taps, double-taps, dragging and pinch zoom elsewhere on the camera pictures remain available. Keyboard users can Tab to the playback tab and activate it with Enter or Space.

The tab, drawer and compact playback indicator sit 4 CSS pixels above the tallest bottom-row footer, adjusting when its text wraps or the layout changes. Footer controls such as **Check now** and **Audio** remain clickable. The camera grid keeps its size when the drawer opens or closes. The revealed tab retracts when the mouse leaves or after three seconds of touch inactivity. The drawer fades away after three seconds of inactivity and stays open while hovered, dragging a control, or using the controls with the keyboard. Select the **Pin** icon at the right end of the bar to keep it open, or **Unpin** to allow automatic hiding. Closing the drawer restores the small edge tab. All icon buttons have accessible names and hover tooltips. **Escape** closes the revealed tab or drawer when keyboard focus is inside it, before changing the camera zoom or focused view.

The drawer uses the viewer menu's **Overlay transparency** setting and becomes fully readable while hovered, dragging, or focused with the keyboard. Opening the drawer from the tab on a touch screen also makes it readable until it hides. When the drawer is closed during historical playback, a compact **PLAYBACK**, **REVERSE** or **PAUSED** indicator shows signed speed and the delay behind live, with a **Go live** button. The compact **Go live** button remains directly available with mouse, touch or keyboard input. Hiding the drawer leaves playback, sound, and pause unchanged.

The controls are arranged in this order: **Back 10 seconds**, **Play backwards**, **Pause**, **Play forwards**, **Forward 10 seconds**, the existing **Speed ×** number box, then **Slower** (snail), **0.5×**, **1×**, **2×**, **4×**, **Faster** (rabbit), and **Go live**. Lucide icons represent transport, slower/faster and pin actions; reverse play mirrors the forward Play icon. Controls fit in one row at desktop widths and wrap on small screens. The timeline remains below the buttons.

Speed is positive for forward playback, negative for reverse scanning, and **0** while paused. Entering a number selects the matching direction or Pause button. The matching speed preset is highlighted by magnitude; the snail is highlighted below 0.5× and the rabbit above 4× in either direction. Slower halves the current magnitude, Faster doubles it, and presets change magnitude while preserving direction. Selecting a speed while paused resumes the retained direction. Pause stays paused when pressed again; use a Play button to resume.

Switching to backwards play from forward or paused playback starts at **−1×**; switching to forwards play starts at **1×**. Pressing the active Play button again advances to the next preset magnitude, then doubles beyond 4×: **1, 2, 4, 8, 16…** (negative in reverse). Forward speeds are checked against the browser's actual accepted `playbackRate`. A rejected or clamped request leaves the previous speed and selection in place and reports the unsupported request; there is no fixed application maximum. Reverse scanning uses repeated seeks through paused video, rather than negative native playback rates. Its visible cadence depends on media loading and decoding, so it is not smooth frame-by-frame reverse playback.

In the grid, all visible cameras follow one browser clock. In a focused camera, the same controls operate on the visible camera; returning to the grid preserves time, speed and pause. Each browser has independent controls. The ten-second buttons and timeline preserve speed, direction and pause. Reverse scanning pauses at the oldest retained footage, and forward playback catches up to live automatically at the recent edge.

Focus a camera and select **Sound** to hear its buffered audio. Other cameras stay muted. Camera streams without audio remain playable. Reverse scanning is muted and its Sound button is disabled. Forward sound respects pause; the browser may require a click to allow audio. Live **Listen** and push-to-talk are disabled during historical playback. **Go live** restores them. Zoom and picture enhancements also work on buffered video. Current detection boxes are hidden during historical playback to avoid showing present-time observations over old pictures.

Times are shown in the browser's timezone. Each fragment maps its media timestamps to server UTC using its production timestamp, before delivery to viewers. A short window of consistent production intervals estimates source clock pace. Rewind converts the shared UTC cursor back through each fragment's mapping, accounting for different media origins and source pacing. This aligns the server media timeline; unequal delays before fragment production can still misalign the actual scene shown by different cameras. Source pauses beyond the fragment timing uncertainty form missing-footage intervals, and reconnects reset calibration. Saved clips use the same mapping for trimming and playback duration.

Native and browser software live video also follow a shared UTC cursor, including focused sound. The live delay follows the longest participating fragment; cameras that stop delivering data leave synchronization after the live stream's eight-second inactivity window. JPEG previews arrive independently. Production timestamps are a proxy for capture time, not a measurement of sensor-frame alignment. Camera-side encoding, NVR or source buffering and server processing can introduce unequal delays. The time burned into a camera's picture is not used as the application's playback clock; a mismatch between those labels alone does not prove that moving events are out of sync.

## Save replay video

While viewing past footage, press **Save video** to select the start at the current playback time. Playback pauses and the drawer prompts you to choose the end with the timeline, ten-second buttons, or playback controls. Press **Save end** to save the interval. You may select an earlier end: the earlier of the two times becomes the recording start. Select **Cancel selection** to start over. **Save video** is disabled while live. After a successful save, the confirmation appears for ten seconds, then disappears and the controls return to their compact layout.

The grid saves every camera in view; a focused view saves that camera alone. Changing the camera view cancels a pending selection. Returning to live, changing configuration, or disabling buffering also clears the selection. Saved clips appear in **View recordings** with a **Saved live replay** reason and a `replay` trigger source in their metadata. Check **Saved live replay only** to find them, optionally combining this with camera and date filters or saving it as a named filter. The coverage timeline applies the same replay filter. Clips use the same playback, download, protection, deletion, storage budget and retention rules as other recordings. Automatic recording may remain disabled; the recording folder and retention settings still apply.

The save includes whatever retained footage is available within the chosen interval. Cameras with no footage are skipped, and missing footage at either end is omitted, including any start that expires while choosing the end. Reconnects, sleeping cameras and gaps produce separate recordings for each available portion, preserving camera identity and actual times without filling the gaps. A missing-footage error appears only if none of the selected cameras has footage in the interval. Audio is retained when present. Frames are trimmed to the chosen interval rather than rounding the selection to a keyframe. Conversion uses FFmpeg serially and temporary disk space; available disk space and the configured recording storage budget constrain saves.

## Capacity and gaps

Duration and memory are independent retention settings: older complete keyframe fragments leave the buffer when either budget is reached. The menu reports memory actually used. Increasing duration also requires enough memory for the camera bitrate; no memory is allocated in advance. At an average 2 Mbps, video needs approximately 15 MB per camera per minute, plus audio, initialization and metadata. Server working memory, JPEG viewing, FFmpeg and browser decoders need additional resources. Measure CPU and actual bitrate on your host before choosing long windows or buffering many cameras.

Only captured footage is available. Startup fills the buffer gradually. A sleeping camera has no footage until it transmits; rewind does not wake it. Sleep and reconnect gaps display **No buffered footage at this time**. Earlier retained epochs remain playable with their own codec initialization. A paused position that expires moves to the earliest retained position and remains paused. Changing the duration or memory setting starts a fresh cache. Disabling buffering, disconnecting, switching configuration/quality, or restarting the server clears history. Longer-term playback remains available in [View recordings](continuous-recording.md).

Small production-clock gaps between contiguous compressed fragments retain the existing 100 ms timing tolerance. For longer fragments, this expands to the cadence uncertainty accepted by the timing estimator: 10% of the sum of the two neighboring fragments' calibrated wall durations (about 200 ms for two one-second fragments). The previous fixed cutoff could report missing footage only 20–30 seconds behind live despite an uninterrupted compressed stream. Coverage, seek windows and replay exports map these boundaries consistently within the available media; no frames are generated or duplicated. Larger gaps, reconnects and missing compressed frames remain unavailable; dependent frames after missing media become playable at the next independent keyframe. When the cursor leaves a gap and reaches available footage, playback retries immediately instead of retaining the missing-footage cooldown.

The player needs MediaSource or ManagedMediaSource with support for the captured H.264 or H.265 video and AAC audio, as [live listening](live-audio.md) does. Unsupported browsers retain JPEG live viewing. Backward seeking and stepped reverse scanning are supported. Browser playback downloads small keyframe windows rather than the entire retained history and releases players for cameras outside the focused view or while the page is hidden.

Each browser load receives one consistent window containing timing, codec initialization and compressed video. Once the server admits that window, ordinary eviction or startup calibration cannot change it during transfer. Requests already outside retained coverage still fail normally. While the buffer fills, the player keeps downloaded history and extends it as new footage becomes available. Expired read-ahead leaves an already decoded picture playable. Live grid pictures and their retained canvases stay hidden throughout historical playback, including loading and missing-footage states.

## API and access

`GET /api/playback` returns settings, connection generation, retained ranges and memory usage by camera. `PUT /api/playback` saves `{ "enabled": true, "seconds": 3600, "megabytesPerCamera": 1024 }`. Settings changes require administrator access when Integrations authentication is enabled; metadata and media require read access and use the existing same-origin policy.

`GET /api/playback/{channel}/{epoch}?at={unixMilliseconds}` selects the preceding video keyframe and returns a small playlist. Initialization is at `/api/playback/{channel}/{epoch}/init`; fragments are at `/api/playback/{channel}/{epoch}/fragments/{sequence}`. Media that has expired or is unavailable returns HTTP 410. Stream epochs and channel identity are checked independently, so reconnects cannot mix incompatible initialization or footage from other cameras.

The browser uses `GET /api/playback/{channel}/{epoch}/window?at={unixMilliseconds}` for a single admitted load. Its `application/octet-stream` body starts with a four-byte unsigned little-endian JSON header length, followed by the UTF-8 header (`playlist`, `initializationLength`, `fragmentLengths`), initialization bytes, then fragment bytes in playlist order. It retains the same read-access and origin checks. Transfers keep immutable media references until completion or cancellation; compressed video is streamed without a server-side concatenation or base64 copy. The configured buffer budgets still govern retained history, while admitted transfers can keep evicted media alive until their requests finish.

`POST /api/playback/recordings` saves `{ "generation": 1, "start": 1791583200000, "end": 1791583210000, "channels": [1, 2] }`, using Unix milliseconds and the current connection generation. It returns the created ordinary recording entries after conversion and catalogue import complete. The trigger or administrator scope is required when authentication is enabled. Invalid input returns 400; stale playback and selections with no available footage from any selected camera return 409. Partially available intervals are cropped to retained footage, and cameras without footage are skipped. Failed conversion or batch import creates no partial selection in the library. `POST /api/recordings/search` accepts `savedReplayOnly: true`.

## Verification

The Chromium checks require Chrome/Edge/Chromium and FFmpeg on PATH to generate a local video fixture. Set `SCV_TEST_BROWSER` or `SCV_TEST_FFMPEG` to use another executable location.

```text
node --test tests/web-live-rewind.test.cjs
node --test tests/web-live-camera-sync.test.cjs tests/web-camera-video.test.cjs tests/web-camera-frame-clock.test.cjs tests/web-live-media.test.cjs
dotnet run --project tests/SecurityCameraViewer.Web.Tests -c Release -- --camera-sync-only
dotnet run --project tests/SecurityCameraViewer.Web.Tests -c Release -- --rewind-save-only
node --test tests/web-playback-controls.test.cjs tests/test_playback_controls_browser.mjs
dotnet build tests/SecurityCameraViewer.Web.Tests -c Release
dotnet tests/SecurityCameraViewer.Web.Tests/bin/Release/net10.0/SecurityCameraViewer.Web.Tests.dll --rewind-only
dotnet publish src/SecurityCameraViewer.Web -c Release -o artifacts/rewind-web
dotnet tests/SecurityCameraViewer.Web.Tests/bin/Release/net10.0/SecurityCameraViewer.Web.Tests.dll artifacts/rewind-web/SecurityCameraViewer.Web.dll --rewind-integration-only
dotnet run --project tests/SecurityCameraViewer.Recording.Tests -c Release -- --unit-only
```

The integration checks use local generated RTSP footage with and without audio. They verify playable historical fragments, concurrent cameras, longer settings, shared encoder lifetime, gaps, expiry, authorization and independence from permanent recordings. Browser-controller tests cover signed speed, preset and animal selection, repeated play actions, browser speed rejection, reverse scanning and mute, oldest/latest boundaries, focus/grid preservation, asynchronous cleanup and return to live. Real Chromium checks cover icons, control order, responsive layout, keyboard/touch interaction, native speed support and generated buffered video in reverse. Physical camera performance, Linux runtime behavior and mobile browser compatibility still require testing on those devices.
